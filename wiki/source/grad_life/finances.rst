Taxes
-----

Fellowship stipends are taxable, but usually nothing is withheld from them, so the tax comes due
all at once unless you plan ahead. Each topic below links the official rules first, followed by
our understanding as of 2026.

How Fellowships Are Taxed
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**Official rules:** `IRS Topic 421`_ · `IRS Publication 970`_ · `Berkeley 1098-T and fellowship tax info`_

*Our understanding as of 2026:*

- The part of a fellowship that pays for tuition, required fees, and books or supplies required
  for your courses is tax-free. The rest, including the stipend you live on, is taxable income.
- If you're a U.S. citizen or permanent resident, Berkeley doesn't withhold any federal or
  California tax from fellowship payments. Many international students have 14% federal tax
  withheld instead, unless a tax treaty applies (see Berkeley's `foreign payments withholding`_
  page).
- Without withholding, you can end up with a large bill in April, on the order of hundreds to
  thousands of dollars.

Paying Tax on Your Fellowship
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**Official rules:** `IRS estimated tax FAQ`_ · `FTB estimated tax payments`_ · `IRS underpayment penalty`_

*Our understanding as of 2026:* There are three ways to handle tax on fellowship income.

- **Withhold more from your GSI/GSR pay.** If you also have a UC paycheck, add extra withholding
  to your federal W-4 (Step 4(c)) and California DE 4 in UCPath. The IRS
  `Tax Withholding Estimator`_ can work out the amount. Withholding counts as if it were paid
  evenly through the year, so raising it partway through the year still helps. If you withhold
  too much, you get the difference back as a refund.
- **Pay quarterly estimated taxes.** If you're fellowship-only, pay the IRS (`IRS Direct Pay`_)
  and California (`FTB Web Pay`_) directly. Federal payments are due around April 15, June 15,
  September 15, and January 15, usually in four equal parts. California uses the same dates but
  wants 30%, 40%, 0%, and 30% of the year's amount, so nothing is due to California in September.
- **Pay it all when you file in April.** This is allowed, but you may owe an underpayment
  penalty, which works like interest on the payments you skipped. You won't owe one if any of
  these apply:

  - **Federal:** your total tax last year was $0 and you were a U.S. citizen or resident all
    year; you owe less than $1,000 after withholding; or your withholding and on-time payments
    covered at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if your income was over
    $150,000).
  - **California:** last year's California tax after credits and withholding was under $500;
    you had no California tax liability last year; you owe less than $500 this year after
    withholding; or your withholding and on-time payments covered at least 90% of this year's
    tax or 100% of last year's (110% if your income was over $150,000).

  The full exceptions are in the IRS `Form 2210 instructions`_ and FTB `Form 5805 instructions`_.

Tax Forms
^^^^^^^^^

**Official rules:** `Where do I find my W-2?`_ (UCPath) · `Understanding your 1098-T`_ (Berkeley Student Billing)

*Our understanding as of 2026:*

- **W-2:** for GSI/GSR pay, available by January 31, either in UCPath (*Income & Taxes → View
  Online W-2/W-2c*, if you signed up for electronic delivery) or by mail.
- **1098-T:** in CalCentral under *My Finances → Financial Resources*. Box 1 shows tuition and
  fees paid, and Box 5 shows the scholarships and fellowships that went through Berkeley. Box 5
  isn't your taxable amount, and fellowships paid to you directly by an outside funder may not
  be on it. To work out the taxable part, Berkeley points to Worksheet 1-1 in
  `IRS Publication 970`_.

Banking and Direct Deposit
--------------------------

Berkeley pays you through separate systems, and you need to set up direct deposit in each one
or you'll get paper checks:

- **GSI/GSR salary** is paid through UCPath. See `UCPath paycheck options`_ to set up direct
  deposit.
- **Reimbursements and awards** are paid through your student account. Sign up for
  `direct deposit in CalCentral`_ under *My Finances → Financial Resources → Direct Deposit*.

Basic Needs
-----------

The `Basic Needs Center`_ runs the campus food pantry and other support for students. It also
helps students apply for `CalFresh`_, California's food assistance program. Note that a typical
Astronomy PhD salary is above the CalFresh income limit, so most of us don't qualify unless we
have dependents.

Retirement and Investing
------------------------

So you're an adult, now what? Some general advice:

- **Open a Roth IRA as soon as you can.** You can't deduct what you put in, but the growth and
  qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free (see the IRS `Roth IRA page`_). A taxable
  fellowship stipend counts as income you can contribute from, even without a W-2 (see "What Is
  Compensation?" in `IRS Publication 590-A`_). Brokerages like `Fidelity`_ and `Charles Schwab`_
  let you open one online.
- **Invest the money.** Money you put into an IRA isn't invested until you buy something with it.
  Don't leave it as cash, since it loses value to inflation.
- For standard advice on getting started, see the `Bogleheads getting started guide`_.
