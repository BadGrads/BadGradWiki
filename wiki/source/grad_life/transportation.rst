==========================
Getting Around Berkeley
==========================

Berkeley and the Bay Area are easy to navigate without a car. Below are options many graduate students use day to day.
General advice from the University: `Transportation and Safety <https://studentaffairs.berkeley.edu/navigate-berkeley/transportation-and-safety/>`__

Bicycle
-------

There are lots of places to get a bike:

* **Co-ops and community-oriented shops** — for example The Missing Link Bike Shop in Berkeley
* **Used or secondhand** — Craigslist, buy-nothing groups, and people moving out of the area selling their things
* **Traditional bike and outdoor retailers** — Mike's Bikes, REI, Sports Basement, and similar stores
* **Big-box stores** — for example Target

Learn local rules of the road, wear lights and visible gear at night, and use a solid lock and secure parking to reduce theft risk.

Register your bike on campus through `UCPD bicycle and mobility device registration <https://ucpd.berkeley.edu/services/bicycle-and-mobility-device-registration>`__ (often described as licensing); it helps with recovery if the bike is stolen.

Bay Pass
--------
All grad students have `Bay Pass <https://pt.berkeley.edu/public-transportation/baypass-information>`__! It allows you to ride all Caltrain, AC Transit, BART trains, Muni,Trolleys, and Ferries for free~

BART
----

`BART <https://www.bart.gov/>`__ runs across much of the Bay Area. It has train lines that go directory to the airpots including `Oakland International (OAK) <https://www.bart.gov/stations/oakl>`__ and `San Francisco International (SFO) <https://www.bart.gov/stations/sfia>`__ directly.

Bear Transit
------------

`Bear Transit <https://pt.berkeley.edu/bear-transit>`__ is UC Berkeley's campus shuttle system and connects nearby areas to campus.

AC Transit
----------

`AC Transit <https://www.actransit.org/>`__ operates local and regional buses around the East Bay. 

Walking
-------

Walk! Berkeley is a very walkable city.

Car
---

Roughly one third of graduate students have a car, which means about two thirds do not, and it is generally straightforward to get around without one.

If you **do** have a car:

* Some apartments charge extra for a dedicated parking spot, often on the order of $50–100 per month
* The City of Berkeley issues `residential parking permits <https://berkeleyca.gov/city-services/parking/residential-preferential-parking-permits>`__ for overnight on-street parking in eligible zones
* **Campus parking** usually requires a `separate university permit <https://pt.berkeley.edu/StudentParking>`__
* You can also park in the special parking behind Campbell Hall when observing - email Shirlin for a permit.

If you don't own a car but need to drive occasionally, short-term options include `Zipcar <https://www.zipcar.com/>`__ (or the UC Berkeley Zipcar program `here <https://www.zipcar.com/universities/the-university-of-california-berkeley>`__) and `Gig Car Share <https://gigcarshare.com/>`__.

Other options
-------------

Less common but still seen are mopeds (popular with some undergraduates) and motorcycles.

Night Safety
------------

There are a number of resources avaliable through UC Berkeley including people to walk with you at night and private shuttles. See the most up to date resources here: `Night Safety <https://nightsafety.berkeley.edu/>`__
