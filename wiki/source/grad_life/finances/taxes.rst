======================================
Taxes for Berkeley Astronomy Students
======================================

.. admonition:: Disclaimer

   This page contains general information and is not tax advice. Tax laws change, and nothing here
   constitutes legal or financial guidance. Consult a tax professional or the resources listed
   at the bottom of this page for your specific situation.

Graduate student taxes at Berkeley are more complicated than a standard W-2 situation because your
income may come from multiple sources — teaching (GSI), research (GSR), and fellowships — each of
which is taxed differently. The most common surprise for first-year students is that **fellowship
income has no automatic withholding**.

.. list-table:: Common income sources and how they are handled
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 35 35

   * - Income source
     - Withholding?
     - Form(s) received
   * - GSI / GSR (Teaching or Research)
     - Yes — handled via payroll
     - W-2
   * - Fellowship (stipend checks)
     - **No** — you are responsible
     - 1098-T (taxable portion only)
   * - Savings account interest
     - No
     - 1099-INT
   * - Mutual fund / DCP dividends
     - No (tax-deferred in DCP)
     - 1099-DIV (taxable accounts only)

----

Fellowship Taxation
--------------------

Fellowships do **not** have automatic tax withholding, so you receive the full payment each month
but will owe taxes on part of it when you file in April. If your unpaid tax bill exceeds $1,000
for two consecutive years, the IRS charges a penalty for underpayment.

The **taxable portion** of your fellowship is the amount not covered by qualified educational
expenses (tuition and mandatory fees). In practice this is:

- Any fellowship stipend checks you received that are **not** on your W-2
- Minus the value of tuition and mandatory fees paid on your behalf that year

UC maintains a fee schedule each year listing the official tuition and fee amounts, which you can
subtract from the scholarship/grant total on your 1098-T to arrive at your taxable amount.

.. note::

   Health insurance premiums often appear as fellowship income on your 1098-T but are **not**
   taxable. Do not include them when calculating your reportable fellowship income.

----

The 1098-T Form
----------------

Your 1098-T is available each January at `1098t.com <https://www.1098t.com>`__. The online version
contains more detail than the paper copy mailed to you and is worth checking if the numbers
look wrong.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 20 80
   :stub-columns: 1

   * - Box 1 — Payments received
     - Qualified tuition and fees paid on your behalf. This amount is **tax-free**.
   * - Box 5 — Scholarships/grants
     - Total scholarship and grant income received, including fellowship stipends.

The taxable portion of your fellowship is roughly **Box 5 minus Box 1**. Report this on your
federal return as scholarship income. On Form 1040, write ``SCH $AMOUNT`` on the dotted line
next to the wages line so the IRS recognizes it as scholarship income rather than wages.

.. note::

   The university occasionally generates incorrect 1098-T forms. If the numbers do not add up
   against the UC fee schedule and the checks you actually received, you can reconstruct the
   amounts yourself or contact the Graduate Student Affairs Officer for help.

Adjusting Payroll Withholding
-------------------------------

If you receive GSI or GSR pay during the year, the easiest way to cover your fellowship tax
liability is to **increase withholding on your payroll income** so that enough is withheld
throughout the year to cover both income sources.

To adjust your withholding:

1. Log in to `UC At Your Service Online (AYSO) <https://atyourserviceonline.ucop.edu/ayso/>`__
2. Navigate to **Tax Withholdings**
3. Increase your monthly federal (and state) withholding to cover your estimated fellowship
   tax liability

Aiming slightly high is fine — you will receive a refund at filing. DCP and other pre-tax
payroll contributions are already handled on your W-2 and do not require any additional action.

Quarterly Estimated Taxes
---------------------------

If you are on a **fellowship-only** term with no payroll income, there is nothing to withhold
from, so you must pay estimated taxes directly to the IRS and the California FTB each quarter.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 20 80
   :stub-columns: 1

   * - Federal
     - Pay through the `EFTPS (Electronic Federal Tax Payment System) <https://www.eftps.gov/>`__.
       You must register before your first payment; account activation takes a few weeks,
       so set this up early.
   * - California
     - Pay through the `CA Franchise Tax Board web pay portal <https://www.ftb.ca.gov/pay/>`__.
       You will need your California Social Security Number (CSN) to register, which you can
       look up online once you have a prior-year CA return.

Estimated tax due dates are typically mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January.
Check the IRS and FTB websites for exact dates each year.

Tax Help at Berkeley
---------------------

Berkeley has several free resources specifically for graduate students:

.. list-table::
   :widths: 20 80
   :stub-columns: 1

   * - ASUC Student Legal Clinic
     - Offers a free **tax clinic** for Berkeley students each spring through ASUC.
       Check the `ASUC website <https://asuc.org/>`__ for dates and sign-up information.
   * - Graduate Assembly
     - Has periodically organized "How to File Taxes" workshops for grad students.
       Slides from past workshops may be available on the
       `Graduate Assembly website <https://ga.berkeley.edu/>`__.
   * - ISSS Tax Workshops
     - `Services for International Students and Scholars (ISSS) <https://isso.berkeley.edu/>`__
       offers workshops and software recommendations specifically for nonresident filers.
       **International students should use ISSS-recommended software** designed for nonresident
       tax situations, as standard consumer tax software (TurboTax, etc.) is not designed
       for nonresident returns.
   * - IRS Publication 970
     - Covers tax benefits for education, including the official treatment of scholarships
       and fellowships. Available at `irs.gov/publications/p970 <https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970>`__.
