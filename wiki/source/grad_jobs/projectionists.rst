==================================
Colloquium Projectionists
==================================

The department projectionist makes sure that the weekly colloquium speakers can project their talks. 
This involves getting and setting up the projector (if there isn't one already in the room where the talk is to be held), 
ensuring the speaker is setup with a microphone before the talk, maintaining the laser pointers, 
and asking the speaker at tea if there's anything they need. 
(Note: the laser pointer requires 1.5V alkaline batteries, NOT rechargeable batteries (they don't have enough voltage). 
You can use the alkaline batteries in the bag). 
The projectionists should also bring an extra projector (and assorted equipment, if available) to colloquium. 
Finally, it is the responsibility of outgoing projectionists to train the incoming projectionists. 
This requires weekly work (just before colloquium) and is usually held by two first year students per semester.

Transitioning from Colloquium Tea to Colloquium
-----------------------------------------------

The department holds a weekly colloquium tea, for which Shirlin orders light-to-moderate refreshments and 
at which people are generally encouraged to mingle (including the colloquium speaker). 

Colloquium tea runs from 3:00 to 3:30 PM. It's typically advisable for at least one of the colloquium projectionists to leave
colloquium tea by 3:25 PM to collect the Zoom laptop from 501 (currently stored by Jason in 501K) and bring it down to 
the colloquium room (Campbell 131).

Ideally, the speaker's host will bring the speaker to 131 by 3:30 PM. If they do not show up by 3:35 PM, they should be found.
(Colloquium starts promptly at 3:40 PM and people get irritable if it starts later due to A/V difficulties.)

When they arrive, have the speaker connect to the projector via the HDMI cable. 
**Test their slides** (sometimes Presenter View shows up on the projector instead of on their laptop).

Setting Up the Zoom
-------------------

The Zoom laptop comes in a Berkeley-branded tote bag with an instruction sheet on setting up the Zoom and recording to the cloud.
This should be followed --- it's not important who signs in to Zoom; as long as the instructions in the tote bag are followed, 
the recording will be accessible by Jason afterward.

As soon as possible after setting up the Zoom, give the room code to the colloquium speaker and 
have them join the Zoom using their laptop.
Have them **screen-share** but **remain muted**.

.. admonition:: Unmute the Zoom laptop early.

   Then test the mic by speaking at a normal volume from the speaker's podium. Zoom will send a popup to the speaker's laptop 
   (since they're muted) saying something like "You're muted; click here to dismiss," and it will not dismiss itself.
   **You (or the speaker) have to dismiss it.** 

   This has led to the popup remaining on the speaker's screen, not visible to any Zoom attendees, but prominently visible to everyone
   in 131 throughout the talk. 
   It isn't really the biggest deal if this happens, but it's better if it doesn't.

.. admonition:: Hit "Record to cloud" early.

   This will also result in a popup on the speaker's screen, which needs to be dismissed manually before the colloquium begins.


Keep an Eye on the Zoom
-----------------------

During the talk, keep an eye on the Zoom to let in late attendees (and reconnect the mic or video camera if the connection breaks).

After the Talk
--------------

Hit "Stop recording," sign out, and close the laptop. No further action is needed on your end for the recording. 
Either Jason will retrieve it or he won't; usually colloquia are not posted online or distributed anywhere unless the 
speaker specifically asks for the recording.

Bring the laptop back to 501K or, if it's the first Thursday of the month, to one of the Astro Night coordinators.
